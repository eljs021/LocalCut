How LocalCut works

LocalCut removes the background from a photo using an AI model that runs inside your web browser. Your image is never sent to a LocalCut server. This page explains what happens from the moment you pick a file to the moment you download the result.

1. You choose an image

When you select or drop a JPG, PNG or WEBP file, LocalCut first checks it on your device. It looks at the file type, the file size and the number of pixels. Nothing is uploaded during these checks.

2. The tool loads (first time only)

The first time you use LocalCut, your browser downloads the background-removal code and the AI model files it needs. This is why your first image can take a little longer. Your browser keeps these files, so later images usually start much faster.

LocalCut uses an open-source library called @imgly/background-removal. The library code is loaded from the jsDelivr content delivery network, and the AI model files are loaded from IMG.LY's model hosting. These downloads contain the tool itself, not your image.

3. The model finds the subject

The AI model looks at the shapes, edges and colours in your image and decides which pixels belong to the main subject and which belong to the background. It does this using your device's own processor, which is why LocalCut needs no server farm and can stay free.

4. The background becomes transparent

The model's result is applied to your original image, making the background pixels transparent. The image is saved as a PNG because PNG supports transparency. Click Download PNG to save it. The checkerboard pattern behind the preview shows which areas are transparent.

Why your image stays on your device

All of the steps above happen in your browser tab. Your image and the result are held in your device's memory and are cleared when you close or refresh the page. To see exactly how it works, you can read the source code.

LocalCut does display ads and load some resources from third-party providers. See the Privacy Policy for details about what those services may collect.

Size limits

Because processing happens on your own device, very large images can be a problem. The amount of memory an image needs depends mostly on how many pixels it has, not just its file size. LocalCut therefore checks both:

  • Images up to 25MB and 50 megapixels are accepted.
  • Images over 10MB or 16 megapixels show a warning, because they can be slow and may fail on phones or older computers.
  • Images over 25MB or 50 megapixels are refused. Resizing the image first will fix this.

Getting better results

  • Choose a photo where the subject stands out clearly from the background.
  • Good lighting helps. Dark, blurry or low-contrast photos are harder to cut out.
  • Fine details such as hair, fur, glass and thin objects can be imperfect. Check the result before you use it.
  • If an image is slow or fails, try a smaller version of the same photo.
